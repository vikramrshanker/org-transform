Sector map for an AI implementation partner

70 lower middle market service sectors where AI adds margin and a sponsor already owns the platform

Consumer services, healthcare, and business and financial services sectors, ranked within each group for two things: how much AI can change the operating cost and service quality of a founder-owned business, and how likely an outside AI partner is to win the work and deliver it. Each sector lists private equity sponsors that already own companies there, so the outreach list writes itself.

Scope
US companies, roughly $3M to $25M EBITDA, founder or family owned, first or second institutional capital
Coverage
70 sectors (16 consumer services, 30 healthcare, 24 business and financial services), 242 sponsor and portfolio company pairs, 165 distinct sponsors
Use
Two ways to work with a sponsor: AI enablement work across the portfolio, and minority co-investment where the AI plan is central to the thesis
Prepared
September 2026. Sponsor attributions reflect public announcements at that date and change often

AI upside

How much of the cost base and customer experience sits in workflows that current models handle well: intake, documents, scheduling, phone and email volume, coding and compliance. Five means a large share of headcount cost is addressable today.

Partner fit

How likely an outside implementation partner wins and delivers: messy data, thin in-house technology teams, sponsors who need a repeatable playbook across add-ons, and use cases with contained scope and measurable payback. Lower where operators build their own or where software vendors already embed the capability.

Disintermediation risk Low Medium High

Whether AI threatens the business itself rather than helping it. Low means a license, a truck, a facility or a fiduciary role keeps the operator in the value chain. High means the labor being sold is what AI replaces, so the operator has to own the transition or lose to it. High risk sectors are still on the list because that transition is the work.

  • Ownership changes constantly. Several platforms listed here changed hands in 2025 and 2026, and prior owners are noted where the exit is recent. Confirm the current sponsor before a first meeting.
  • Some sponsors run in-house AI engineering teams (Percheron Capital's AI Ops is one example). Position as added capacity and speed rather than as a missing capability.
  • Several sectors include large-cap sponsors for context. The lower middle market targets are the regional platforms and their add-ons, not the national leaders.
  • A few pairs rest on a single trade-press item; these are marked in the basis column. Treat them as leads, not confirmed facts.
  • Nothing here is investment advice. Co-investment decisions need company-level diligence.
