00 / Executive brief

The output gets cheaper before the whole service does.

AI lowers the effort required for many drafts, analyses and software components. That creates price pressure where the output itself is the product. It can also increase spending on the data, integration and accountable delivery needed to put that output to use.

01

Production costs can fall sharply

A randomized writing experiment found 40% less time and 18% higher assessed quality on assigned tasks. That is strong evidence for those tasks, not the economics of an entire professional firm.[2]

02

Substitution is visible in some businesses

Chegg’s 2025 revenue fell 39% to $376.9m; management described continuing search-interface pressure. The decline is real, but its causes are not isolated.[5]

03

Incumbents can capture the new spending

Accenture reported $2.7bn of generative/agentic AI revenue in FY2025. Thomson Reuters and Salesforce also report growing AI offerings. Distribution and workflow position still matter.[6], [8], [9]

04

Price the accepted outcome

Generation, checking, revision, integration and support all consume resources. The defensible product must do something valuable after the first plausible answer exists.

Meaning of commoditization. Here it means customers can obtain a sufficiently similar output from more suppliers at lower cost. It does not imply zero prices, no differentiation or the disappearance of all software and service providers.
01 / Production evidence

Cheaper generation is established. Universal productivity is not.

The economic chain has several links: model capability → cost of producing a usable artifact → cost of delivering an accepted outcome → customer price → provider margin. Evidence at the first or second link cannot settle the last three. Competition, contracts, distribution and switching costs determine how the benefit is divided.

02 / Company evidence

The same technology can erode one business and expand another.

Education / Company financial results / Calendar 2025

Chegg: pressure on an answer product and its acquisition channel

$376.9m2025 total net revenue
−39%year-over-year revenue change
$103.4m2025 net loss

What happened

Chegg’s February 2026 results show a sharp contraction. Management described continued traffic pressure from changes in search interfaces and a reorganization around skills training. Q4 total revenue was $72.7m, down 49% year over year.[5]

What it suggests

A business can face both a cheaper substitute for its core output and a weaker acquisition channel. Improving generation inside the product may not repair either source of pressure. Distinctive outcomes, distribution or customer relationships become central.

Evidence boundary. This is one company in a changing market. The financial results do not allocate the decline among AI answers, search behavior, pricing, product choices and other factors.

Professional services / FY2025

Accenture: cheaper analysis can create implementation demand

$2.7bngenerative/agentic AI revenue
3×that revenue versus FY2024
$5.9bngenerative AI bookings

What happened

Accenture’s FY2025 account reports growing AI revenue and bookings, excluding data, classical AI and AI used in delivery. It also reports $1bn invested in learning and development and roughly 47m training hours across its workforce.[6]

What it suggests

A customer buying organizational change needs more than a generated recommendation. Existing client relationships, integration capability and operating responsibility can support demand even as some billable tasks get faster. A smaller entrant needs a specific advantage within that broader work.

Evidence boundary. The figures are company-defined categories. Bookings are future contracted activity, not recognized sales; neither metric establishes AI project margins or proves customers achieved a return.

Professional information / Calendar 2025–February 2026

Thomson Reuters: authoritative content inside the workflow

7%2025 organic revenue growth
9%organic growth in the “Big 3” segments
1mreported CoCounsel users in February 2026

What happened

Thomson Reuters reported 3% total revenue growth and 7% organic growth in 2025. Its three core professional segments grew 9% organically. In February 2026 it announced one million CoCounsel users across 107 countries and territories.[7], [8]

What it suggests

Trusted reference material, customer distribution and integration with professional work can remain valuable when generic drafting improves. The test is whether the combined product makes a consequential task easier to complete and verify.

Evidence boundary. Company growth is not all attributable to AI. The user figure does not disclose active paid seats, retention or customer economics. The case challenges universal commoditization; it does not prove a permanent moat.

Enterprise software / FY2026

Salesforce: separate AI demand from acquired revenue

$800mAgentforce ARR
+169%Agentforce ARR year-over-year growth
$1.1bnInformatica Cloud ARR inside the combined figure

What happened

Salesforce’s February 2026 release reports Agentforce ARR of $800m. Its broader Agentforce and Data 360 ARR exceeds $2.9bn, but includes $1.1bn from Informatica Cloud. The two figures answer different questions.[9]

What it suggests

Installed workflows and access to customer data may give incumbents a distribution advantage in selling agents. An entrant’s comparison must be against the incumbent’s evolving offering, not an assumed static legacy product.

Evidence boundary. ARR is not revenue collected during the fiscal year, and the combined figure is not all organic AI growth. Adoption, use, customer ROI and supplier margin remain separate measures.

03 / Where value remains

Ask what the customer still needs after the draft.

A value-chain view of software and service exposure
LayerPressure from cheaper generationPotential source of durable value
Generic content and first-pass analysisMore substitutes, easier self-service and lower production effort.Distinctive evidence, original reporting or a direct audience willing to pay.
Application constructionFaster creation of interfaces, scripts and routine features.Reliability, integration, maintenance, data continuity and an established place in daily work.
Business-process deliveryAutomation can remove steps and lower staffing requirements.A measurable outcome, exception handling, process ownership and continuing adaptation.
Professional judgmentResearch and drafting may accelerate.Context, verification, licensure where relevant, responsibility and client trust.
Systems of record and distributionNew interfaces may sit above existing applications.Authoritative data, transaction execution, permissioned access and customer relationships.

These are hypotheses about value capture, not protections guaranteed to an incumbent. A system of record can become easier to replace if migration improves. A service firm can lose pricing power if clients demand the same work for less. A trusted brand can fail if the product ceases to deliver a distinctive result.

A useful test is to remove the model from the description of a proposed company. What remains? A unique dataset with rights to use it, a process customers already depend on, a distribution channel, a service guarantee or a scarce capability would each be a concrete answer. “We generate the same report faster” offers a less durable starting point unless speed changes a valuable decision.

04 / Delivery and pricing

Move from selling effort to defining the result.

Intercom advertises Fin at $0.99 per outcome alongside its broader platform pricing. Its billing definitions can count a resolution when the customer confirms help or leaves without asking for more. This is a concrete shift toward charging for a result, while also showing why the contractual definition matters.[10], [11]

Outcome pricing moves risk rather than abolishing it. If a provider charges a flat amount per accepted case, it bears more of the cost of difficult cases and rework. If the definition is too weak, it can bill for apparent completion while the customer still has a problem. If too narrow, necessary work falls outside the contract. A useful agreement needs a clear boundary and an exception path.

This also changes organization. Less time may go into first drafts; more into scoping, review, integration and customer handling. Management must decide whether to reduce price, increase output, improve quality or retain margin. Those choices have different staffing and sales implications even with the same technology.

05 / Unit economics

A 90% drop in model cost may barely move the cost of service.

Illustration: cost per accepted business case
Cost componentInitial designModel 90% cheaperWorkflow redesigned
Model and tools$2.00$0.20$0.20
Human review$12.00$12.00$4.00
Integration and support allocation$6.00$6.00$3.00
Expected rework$5.00$5.00$2.00
Total cost per accepted case$25.00$23.20$9.20
Contribution at an assumed $35 price$10.00$11.80$25.80

All values are assumptions. Model deflation alone reduces the initial $25 cost by 7.2%. The redesigned workflow reduces it by 63.2%, but only if the review and rework assumptions hold at the same acceptance standard. At a competitive price of $15, the redesigned process contributes $5.80 while the unchanged process loses $8.20 per case. Sales, general overhead and one-time build costs are excluded.

A customer deciding whether to build or buy should make the same comparison over the system’s life. Cheap initial construction can be attractive for a small internal tool. A workflow involving changing policies, multiple integrations or high failure costs can make maintenance and accountability dominate. Neither “software is dead” nor “switching costs protect everything” is a useful universal assumption.

06 / Opportunity hypotheses

Own a scarce part of the completed outcome.

Candidate openings linked to the brainstorms
OpeningWhy it might support a businessDisproof test
Verified analysis in a narrow domainCombine authoritative evidence, reproducible calculations and a decision-specific workflow.A generic assistant plus existing data sources delivers equivalent accepted work.
Managed executionTake responsibility for a recurring process, with AI reducing delivery cost.Exception labor, customer support or liability prevents attractive margins.
Integration and maintenanceKeep changing tools connected to actual business systems and operating rules.Work remains bespoke, or an incumbent bundles the integration.
Evidence and quality infrastructureMeasure accepted outcomes, failures and financial effects across a workflow.The customer will not act on the measurement or existing tools already provide it.
Acquire a service business and change deliveryRetain operating gains inside an existing customer relationship.Competition rapidly passes savings to customers, or acquisition price consumes the upside.

The distinction between a company, a service and a feature remains open. A narrow report generator may be a useful feature. A repeatable service that owns a costly outcome can be a business even if its software is easy to reproduce. Conversely, a technically impressive platform may struggle if the buyer has little recurring need.

07 / What would change the view

Track substitution, pricing and accepted outcomes together.

Evidence needed to judge the opportunity
QuestionMeasureThesis weakens if…
Is the customer substituting?Win/loss interviews, actual purchasing changes and reasons for churn.Users experiment with AI but retain the existing service at the same price.
How much cost is really removed?Total labor, model, integration and rework cost per accepted outcome.Savings disappear at the required quality or on difficult cases.
Who retains the benefit?Price changes, gross contribution and competitive alternatives over time.Customers capture all savings while delivery risk stays with the supplier.
What is difficult to reproduce?Evidence rights, distribution, operating integration and customer dependence.A platform feature or inexpensive service can reproduce the benefit.
Is it repeatable?Paid delivery across multiple customers, with measured setup and maintenance effort.Each sale recreates a consulting project with no improving economics.

The strongest conclusion is about the direction of competition: production alone becomes a less reliable basis for pricing. The pace differs by task and business model. A promising company should be able to explain exactly which part of the outcome remains scarce—and demonstrate that customers pay for it.

  1. Epoch AI: inference price trends ↗
    March 12, 2025

    Independent analysis of model prices at fixed benchmark performance. Historical rates vary by benchmark and period. Token prices exclude integration, review and the cost of an accepted business outcome.

  2. Noy & Zhang: productivity effects of generative AI ↗
    Science, July 14, 2023

    Randomized experiment; 453 professionals on writing tasks. Short assigned work products and evaluator scores, not whole-job productivity or firm profit. Published abstract and institutional summary reviewed.

  3. METR: early-2025 developer productivity experiment ↗
    July 2025; February–June 2025 tools

    Randomized task access; 16 developers, 246 tasks. Experienced contributors working in familiar mature projects. A narrow historical result, not a current estimate for all developers.

  4. METR: changes to developer experiment design ↗
    February 24, 2026

    Primary follow-up and methodological analysis. Later observations suffered selection and measurement problems. The researchers do not provide a dependable universal current speedup estimate.

  5. Chegg: 2025 full-year results ↗
    February 9, 2026; year ended December 31, 2025

    Company financial results and management commentary. Financial decline is observed; the contribution of AI substitution versus distribution and other changes is not experimentally identified.

  6. Accenture: FY2025 financial performance ↗
    FY2025, ended August 31, 2025

    Company-reported revenue, bookings and investment. AI classifications are management-defined. Bookings are not recognized revenue or profit; company performance does not establish the whole market.

  7. Thomson Reuters: 2025 full-year results ↗
    February 5, 2026; calendar 2025

    Public-company results, with organic-growth reconciliation. Growth includes more than AI. Organic and reported revenue growth are different metrics; no isolated causal AI profit measure.

  8. Thomson Reuters: one million CoCounsel users ↗
    February 24, 2026

    Vendor-reported product reach. Reported users are not a disclosed paid-active-user cohort, retention measure or customer productivity estimate.

  9. Salesforce: Q4 and FY2026 results ↗
    February 25, 2026; fiscal year ended January 31

    Company results and product ARR disclosures. ARR is a run-rate metric, not period revenue. The combined Agentforce/Data 360 figure includes acquired Informatica Cloud ARR.

  10. Intercom: pricing ↗
    Price page checked September 21, 2026

    Vendor’s advertised commercial terms. List price per outcome is not the full customer cost. Eligibility, plan charges and negotiated terms can differ.

  11. Intercom: pricing FAQs and outcome definition ↗
    Documentation checked September 21, 2026

    Provider definitions of billable events. A billed resolution can include a customer leaving without requesting more help; it is not independently verified customer success.

  12. Google Cloud: 2025 DORA findings ↗

    Primary survey research summary; nearly 5,000 technology professionals. Associations between AI use and delivery outcomes are not randomized causal estimates. Publisher supplies development tools.
