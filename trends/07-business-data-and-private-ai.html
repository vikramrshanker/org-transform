Business data, access rights and private AI

Useful AI depends on the right information reaching the right person—and staying within the right boundaries.

00 / Executive brief

Private AI is a system property, not a server location.

The valuable input is not simply a pile of company documents. It is current, relevant information the business has authority to use, with permissions that survive retrieval, generation and action. That work can be necessary whether the model runs locally or in a managed cloud.

01

Internal access is a real boundary

Microsoft says Copilot operates within existing permissions, while warning that overshared content increases risk. Making information easier to retrieve can expose weaknesses in the original permission design.[4]

02

The security evidence needs its denominator

IBM’s 2026 study covered 602 breached organizations. Roughly one in five reported an attack on AI models or applications; 92% of that subgroup lacked proper AI access controls. This is not 92% of all businesses.[1], [2]

03

Regulated firms already use hosted AI

Morgan Stanley and BBVA describe enterprise deployments with internal processes, consent, training and review. Their experience challenges the assumption that sensitive work always requires owning the hardware.[7], [8]

04

The commercial gap is operational

Rights, permissions, freshness, evaluation and integration can remain difficult after model access is purchased. Whether that gap supports a company depends on repeatable delivery and an identifiable buyer.

Scope. This report examines data access, architecture and operating economics. It separates model training, inference, retrieval and agent actions. Regulatory sources establish relevant constraints, not a legal conclusion about a particular dataset or deployment.
01 / Observed demand and risk

Use and control do not automatically advance together.

What the evidence establishes—and what it does not
ObservationFindingBoundary
BYOAI, 2024[3]78% of AI-using knowledge workers in Microsoft/LinkedIn’s survey brought their own tools to work.Historical self-report among users. Bringing a tool does not necessarily mean exposing protected data.
Breached organizations, 2026 study[1], [2]About one in five reported an AI-model or application breach; 92% of that subgroup lacked proper AI access controls.Selected breached organizations. Cannot estimate an ordinary business’s breach probability.
AI-enabled attacks, same study[2]One quarter of malicious breaches were AI-enabled; average associated cost was $6m.Attacker use of AI is distinct from an organization’s AI system being attacked. Large-sample averages are not an SME loss forecast.
Existing enterprise systems[4], [5]Copilot honors permissions; its interactions and referenced content can also enter retention and audit systems.Private processing does not mean no stored records. The deployment includes more than the model endpoint.

The implication is narrower than “every company needs private AI.” A business needs an environment appropriate to the data and action. Public information summarization, confidential document search and autonomous payment execution have different failure modes. Buying the same infrastructure for all three can waste money while leaving the consequential boundary unaddressed.

We did not find a single comparable public dataset measuring how many ordinary businesses require on-premise inference, what they will pay for it or whether it improves outcomes relative to a suitable hosted service. Vendor market forecasts do not fill that gap. The company thesis therefore rests on identifiable workloads and purchasing evidence, not a global “private AI” market estimate.

02 / Deployment cases

The operating boundary matters more than the label.

Financial services / June 2024

Morgan Stanley: consent, advisor review and the system of record

98%advisor teams adopting the earlier Assistant
Client consentrequired for meeting-note generation
Advisor reviewbefore the drafted email is sent

What happened

Morgan Stanley’s Debrief announcement describes meeting summaries and action items generated with client consent, an email the advisor can edit and send, and notes saved into Salesforce. The reported 98% adoption figure belongs to the earlier knowledge Assistant.[7]

What it suggests

The workflow connects information capture to a retained record while preserving a person’s responsibility for communication. Useful private deployment involves consent, retrieval, editing and recordkeeping together; a separate model contract is only one component.

Evidence boundary. The announcement does not disclose controlled time savings, Debrief adoption or a security-outcome comparison. Its earlier Assistant adoption figure must not be assigned to the new tool.

Bank-wide enablement / 2025 account

BBVA: adoption support accompanied enterprise access

3,300 → 11,000enterprise licenses in the expansion
>80%reported daily use among licensed users
2.8 hoursweekly time savings reported by task-automating users

What happened

BBVA describes a wider license rollout following its initial May 2024 deployment. Training, a community for sharing practice and internal idea events accompanied access. Its adoption team collected the employee time-saving reports.[8]

What it suggests

A managed environment can be part of a scaled operating program. The organizational work is helping staff identify useful tasks, learn acceptable use and share tested practice. Access alone does not supply those capabilities.

Evidence boundary. Self-reported time is not measured cost reduction. The user subset, daily-use measure and license count are different denominators. This 2025 case is not the bank’s current deployment census.

Privacy architecture / 2024 design and June 2026 extension

Apple: make the trust boundary inspectable

2024Private Cloud Compute architecture introduced
2026expansion beyond Apple data centers announced
Verifiablesoftware and infrastructure claims central to design

What happened

Apple’s architecture aims to process private requests without persistent user-data storage or privileged access to the request. It emphasizes software transparency and attestation. In June 2026 Apple described extending PCC onto Google Cloud infrastructure while retaining control of PCC software.[9], [10]

What it suggests

The relevant distinction is what the infrastructure operator can access and what the client can verify. Physical ownership of the data center alone does not describe those properties. A business should ask for evidence about its actual data path and operating controls.

Evidence boundary. These are Apple’s architectural claims. The June extension described a preview ramp toward the full protections, not a completed universal rollout. PCC is an illustrative platform architecture, not a generic enterprise purchasing option.

03 / Data and rights

Possession, permission and usefulness are different tests.

Five questions before a dataset becomes an AI asset
QuestionWhat to establishFailure if ignored
May we use it for this purpose?Contractual scope, confidentiality, personal-data obligations and applicable licenses.A company holds a file but cannot reuse it for a new purpose or across customers.
Who may see it?Identity, role, customer, project and document-level permissions.A retrieval system uses a broad service account and reveals information the user should not receive.
Is it current and authoritative?Version, effective date, owner and relationship to the source system.A persuasive answer uses an expired price, old procedure or superseded agreement.
Can we remove or correct it?Propagation into indexes, caches, logs, derived records and any trained artifacts.Changing the original document leaves usable copies elsewhere.
Does it improve the result?A representative comparison with and without the data, at equal quality standards.An expensive collection adds noise without improving accepted outcomes.

The EDPB’s December 2024 opinion treats model anonymity case by case and describes a three-step assessment for legitimate interest. An “internal” label does not supply a lawful basis. The EU Data Act, generally applicable since September 2025, creates access and sharing rights for certain connected-product data; it does not make all commercial data freely reusable.[11], [12]

Our interpretation: a durable data advantage needs both utility and authority. Exclusive access to irrelevant records is not a moat. Useful information available on the same terms to every competitor may improve the product without differentiating the company. A strong position would combine permissioned access, continuing freshness, outcome feedback and a workflow that makes the information valuable.

For acquisition diligence, access may also be temporary and purpose-limited. A prospective buyer’s permission to inspect records is not automatically permission to train a reusable model or retain other parties’ data after a failed deal. A proposed service must resolve those permissions explicitly in its actual agreements.

04 / Architecture choices

Choose the boundary before the hardware.

Deployment approaches and their economic tradeoffs
ApproachWhat it can provideWhat still needs work
Enterprise applicationReady-made interface, administrative controls and existing workflow integration.Data readiness, correct permissions, retention configuration, evaluation and supplier dependence.
Managed model serviceProvider-operated models with enterprise data terms and configurable network access.The customer’s application, logs, connectors, access checks and operating procedures.
Dedicated or private-cloud deploymentMore control over isolation, configuration and sometimes location.Capacity planning, operations, model updates and the accuracy of the surrounding access design.
On-premise or device inferenceLocal processing, possible offline use and direct control of infrastructure.Hardware utilization, patching, model quality, physical and logical security, and staff capability.

AWS states that Bedrock does not share customer data with model providers or use it to improve base models, and supports private connectivity through PrivateLink. This is evidence that enterprise privacy controls can exist in a managed service. It is not a claim that every log, application component or integrated service has zero retention.[6]

Retrieval and fine-tuning also solve different problems. Retrieval supplies selected source material at use time; fine-tuning changes model behavior using training examples. A permission-sensitive document collection often needs query-time access checks and source updates. Encoding documents into model weights does not create a document-level permission system. OWASP also warns that retrieval and fine-tuning do not fully mitigate prompt injection.[13]

This work needs ownership beyond IT. The business decides which information is authoritative and which actions are permitted; security defines and tests boundaries; operations handles exceptions; someone funds ongoing upkeep. NIST’s generative-AI profile offers a framework for organizing such risks, but adopting a framework is not proof that a deployment performs well.[14]

05 / Economics

Volume and quality determine the infrastructure tradeoff.

The comparison below is deliberately hypothetical. A managed service costs $2,000 per month in fixed integration and operations plus $0.06 per accepted task. A self-hosted alternative costs $14,000 per month including assumed hardware amortization, staffing and operations, plus $0.01 per accepted task. Both are assumed to achieve the same quality, latency and availability; that assumption must be tested.

Illustrative monthly total cost at different volumes
Accepted tasks / monthManaged serviceSelf-hostedLower modeled cost
10,000$2,600$14,100Managed, by $11,500
100,000$8,000$15,000Managed, by $7,000
500,000$32,000$19,000Self-hosted, by $13,000

The model’s break-even volume is ($14,000 − $2,000) / ($0.06 − $0.01) = 240,000 accepted tasks per month. This is arithmetic, not a hardware benchmark or vendor quote. A deployment may cross a capacity limit before that point, need redundant machines or require more expensive models. Unequal quality invalidates the comparison.

06 / Opportunity hypotheses

Make specific data usable for a valuable workflow.

Potential openings and the likely buyer
NeedPossible businessWhat would weaken the case
Permission-aware deployment / IT and business ownerConfigure access, connectors, retention and evaluations for one repeatable workflow.The existing enterprise application solves the problem with modest internal effort.
Maintained operating knowledge / business owner or acquirerKeep procedures, exceptions and source evidence current through a transition.Content is rarely used, cannot be shared legally or requires expensive manual upkeep.
Rights and provenance / data supplier or workflow operatorTrack which information may be used where and preserve source lineage.No recurring decision depends on the extra record, or existing contracts and catalog tools suffice.
Managed private workflow / regulated or sensitive-data operatorDeliver a bounded outcome with ongoing review, access administration and support.Customization or liability prevents profitable repeat delivery.
Specialized local inference / constrained operatorSupport workloads with genuine offline, latency, location or isolation requirements.Those constraints are preferences rather than funded requirements, or utilization is too low.

Established cloud, identity and enterprise-software providers already supply much of the foundation. A generic “private chatbot” faces bundling pressure. An entrant needs a concrete constraint they do not solve economically: unusual permissions, legacy data, a specialized operating process or a demonstrable local-processing requirement.

For the brainstorms’ private-AI and knowledge-capture ideas, the stronger hypothesis is a maintained operational service with software. It would sell a useful and bounded result, not merely access to a model or a collection of GPUs. That could become recurring revenue if the underlying information and decisions keep changing. The evidence here does not yet establish that customers will pay enough for the upkeep.

07 / What would change the view

Test the data advantage and the delivery burden.

Evidence that would sharpen the company thesis
QuestionUseful testThesis weakens if…
Is private deployment actually required?Document the buyer’s concrete contractual, operational or technical constraint and compare acceptable architectures.An existing managed product meets the requirement cheaply.
Does the data change performance?Blind comparison on representative cases with and without company data, including stale and conflicting records.Generic context performs equally well or reviewers correct most answers.
Do permissions hold?Test users with different roles, revoked access, cross-customer separation and adversarial documents.The design relies on the model choosing not to reveal information it already received.
Can upkeep be repeated?Track connector failures, access changes, source refresh work and expert review hours across paid customers.Maintenance expands linearly with custom effort and consumes the margin.
Does the buyer get a business result?Accepted outcomes, cycle time, total human effort, errors and retained use.Privacy is the sales story but no valuable workflow is improved.

Revisit the architecture when model quality, service terms, volume or operating requirements change. The more durable asset may be the workflow, its permissioned data and its evidence of reliable outcomes; the hosting choice can remain replaceable.

    Primary architecture and retention documentation. Prompts, responses and referenced content can create auditable and retained records. Actual behavior depends on service and configuration.

    Documentation checked September 21, 2026

    Primary managed-cloud service commitments. No base-model training and private connectivity are distinct from zero retention across a customer’s application, logs and integrated services.

  7. Morgan Stanley: Debrief launch ↗

    Deploying institution’s workflow and adoption account. 98% refers to teams adopting the earlier Assistant, not Debrief usage, productivity or independent security validation.

  8. BBVA: expansion to 11,000 enterprise licenses ↗

    Bank-reported adoption and employee time estimates. Self-reported savings among task-automating users, not measured firm-wide cash savings; license count is not proof of valuable usage.

  9. Apple: Private Cloud Compute architecture ↗
    June 10, 2024

    First-party security architecture. Architecture and inspectability claims, not independent proof that every deployment is invulnerable. Consumer platform, not a general enterprise product.

  10. Apple: expanding Private Cloud Compute ↗
    June 8, 2026

    First-party account of expansion onto Google Cloud infrastructure. The announcement describes a summer preview ramp toward the full protections. It is not evidence that every planned protection was deployed on the announcement date.

  11. EDPB: Opinion 28/2024 on AI models ↗

  12. European Commission: Data Act explained ↗

    Official explanation of connected-product data access and sharing. Scope, safeguards and phased obligations matter. It is not a general right to use every company dataset or override personal-data law.

  13. OWASP: 2025 Top 10 for LLM applications ↗
    2025 edition

    Expert security guidance. Threat taxonomy and design guidance, not frequency estimates. Retrieval and fine-tuning do not eliminate prompt injection.

  14. NIST: generative AI risk-management profile ↗

    Voluntary risk-management framework. A framework for identifying and evaluating risks, not a product certification, compliance verdict or measured business outcome.
