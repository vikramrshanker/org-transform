The constraint is often experienced judgment: how to preserve it, distribute it and develop the next generation.

00 / Executive brief

More output today can coexist with a weaker skills pipeline.

Labor scarcity, loss of company knowledge and difficulty developing new experts are related problems. They require different interventions. AI can help workers use knowledge faster while also reducing the practice through which they acquire it.

01

Hiring pressure remains uneven

In August 2026, 35% of NFIB respondents reported unfilled openings, down one point from July. A global ManpowerGroup survey found 72% reporting difficulty, down from 74% in 2025. These are different populations and questions.[1], [2]

02

Replacement needs exceed net growth

BLS projects 72,700 electrician openings per year but 75,900 net additional jobs across the whole 2025–35 decade. Most projected openings therefore reflect transfers and exits.[3]

03

Expertise can be distributed

A published support study found 15% more issues resolved per hour with AI assistance, with uneven gains across workers. Industrial copilots are also embedding support into engineering work.[9], [10]

04

Learning needs its own measurement

In a small randomized coding study, immediate quiz scores averaged 50% with AI and 67% without. Faster task completion and skill development cannot be assumed to move together.[11]

Reading guide. Official projections describe future demand under BLS assumptions. Employer surveys describe reported difficulty. Experiments and deployment accounts describe particular workflows. Our proposed organizational designs and opportunity hypotheses follow the evidence.
01 / Demand and supply

There is no single labor shortage.

Home health and personal care aides[5]$35,800847,300 / 18%760,500

Openings include workers changing occupations and leaving the labor force as well as newly created positions. They are not current vacancies and cannot all be attributed to retirement. Pay, geography, working conditions, scheduling and licensure can each create a hiring constraint even when people with relevant skills exist.

ILO–NASK estimates that about one quarter of global employment is in occupations with some generative-AI exposure, rising to 34% in high-income countries. Exposure indicates tasks a technology may affect. It does not establish whether employers will automate them, whether demand will grow or whether a worker will lose a job.[8]

A useful diagnosis begins at the local workflow. A field-service firm may lack licensed technicians, yet spend their time on avoidable travel and paperwork. An accounting firm may have enough junior staff but too few reviewers. A care provider may have trained applicants but shifts they cannot reliably cover. Calling all three “skills shortages” conceals the economically relevant constraint.

02 / Evidence from practice

Assistance, industrial deployment and learning produce different results.

Published workplace study / Customer support / 2025

Distribute useful practice at the point of work

15%average increase in issues resolved per hour
One settingcustomer-support organization
Staggeredrollout rather than individual randomization

What happened

Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond studied an assistant that supplied real-time suggestions to support workers. The published result is a 15% average productivity increase, with substantial differences across workers and particularly useful support for less experienced workers.[9]

What it suggests

An organization can make useful patterns from prior work available during a live interaction. The practical question is which knowledge can be captured in that way, and when the worker should seek a specialist instead. Resolution quality and repeat contacts must remain visible.

Evidence boundary. A bounded support workflow does not establish transfer of physical craft, customer trust or all forms of expert judgment. The finding is not a 15% staffing-reduction recommendation.

Supplier deployment account / Industrial engineering / 2024

Siemens: put assistance inside the engineering environment

100+reported customer companies in Europe and the US
30 secreported panel-visualization creation time
20%reported adaptation needed for generated code

What happened

Siemens’ October 2024 account describes an assistant integrated with its engineering tools. Thyssenkrupp Automation Engineering used it in a battery quality-inspection machine. It supported TIA Portal project creation and control-code development; broader rollout was then planned for 2025.[10]

What it suggests

Domain integration matters: the assistant works near the code, configuration and terminology that engineers already use. That can reduce search and drafting effort. Commissioning, safety judgment and physical verification remain separate work.

Evidence boundary. The metrics are supplier claims without a disclosed representative task sample. “20% adaptation” is not an 80% reduction in total engineering labor. The stated 2025 rollout plan is not evidence it was completed.

Randomized experiment / Coding / January 2026

Producing the answer is different from learning the system

52mostly junior engineers in the experiment
50%mean quiz score with AI assistance
67%mean quiz score without AI assistance

What happened

Participants learned an unfamiliar Python library, completed coding tasks and took an immediate comprehension quiz. The AI group’s score was 17 percentage points lower. Its completion-time advantage was small and not statistically significant. More explanatory styles of use were associated with better understanding.[11]

What it suggests

A training process should require explanation and unaided performance as well as a completed artifact. Otherwise a manager can observe higher output while missing a weaker ability to debug, detect mistakes or work without assistance.

Evidence boundary. A short task and immediate test in one technical domain cannot establish long-run deskilling. The experiment randomized AI access, not the different styles of interacting with it.

03 / The skills pipeline

The accounting data contain both a problem and a recovery signal.

AICPA reports 55,152 accounting bachelor’s and master’s degrees in 2023–24, down 6.6% from the prior year. Yet its July 2026 account reports undergraduate accounting enrollment up 7.3% in fall 2025, the third consecutive year of growth. Graduates measure the near-term supply reaching employers; enrollment gives a different, earlier signal.[6], [7]

Avoid three misleading inferences
ObservationTempting inferenceMore precise reading
11,985 new graduates hired by responding accounting firms in 2024.[6]Only that many graduates found jobs nationally.The firm response rate was too low for a national estimate. This is the respondents’ total.
BLS expects accounting employment to grow 5% through 2035.[4]AI has no effect on accounting work.A growing occupation can still change its task mix, staffing pyramid, pricing and skills.
In the 2025 ORS, 70.7% of accountant and auditor jobs required prior experience; 74.1% required on-the-job training.[12]A degree alone makes a worker ready for every accounting role.Formal education, prior experience and employer training are distinct requirements. These percentages do not measure onboarding duration.

Our interpretation: automating routine preparation can make scarce reviewers more productive, but it can also remove the cases through which juniors learn. The organization then needs an explicit apprenticeship design. Junior staff should still encounter representative cases, explain their reasoning, receive correction and demonstrate unaided judgment before taking greater authority.

The constraint may move rather than disappear. If ten preparers generate twice as many files while two reviewers retain the same capacity, the queue grows. Measuring preparation speed alone would reward the wrong intervention. The relevant outcome is completed, accepted work per unit of total team effort.

04 / Knowledge transfer

Capture decisions and exceptions, then test a successor.

Different knowledge needs different treatment
Knowledge typeExampleAppropriate transfer mechanism
Documented informationA product specification, service history or policy.Search and retrieval with current sources, dates and access control.
Conditional operating ruleWhen to discount a quote or replace a component.Worked cases, explicit exceptions, decision logs and expert validation.
Physical or perceptual skillRecognizing a fault from sound, feel or an unusual installation.Supervised practice and observed performance; written guidance is only part of the transfer.
Relationship knowledgeWhich customer will accept a substitution and who must approve it.Introductions, participation in real interactions and explicit authority changes.
Uncertain judgmentA novel failure or ambiguous compliance issue.Escalation to a qualified person; preserve uncertainty rather than manufacture a confident rule.

Participation also needs a reason. The expert may perceive documentation as extra work or as preparation for replacement. A credible program gives them time, recognition and a role in deciding what should be standardized. It also distinguishes proven practice from an individual’s habits. Encoding a bad shortcut can make an organization consistently wrong faster.

These are organizational design implications, not a claim that every company needs a new knowledge platform. Existing field-service, learning and document systems may suffice. A separate product must prove that it improves performance in the work itself.

05 / Economics

Released time is valuable only if the bottleneck moves.

Consider an explicitly hypothetical service team: 20 technicians each spend five hours a week on avoidable administration across 48 working weeks. Removing 40% of that time releases 1,920 hours a year. That is a capacity measure, not payroll savings.

1,152 × $80 contribution per additional productive hour$92,160 annual contributionRequires incremental demand; rate is after variable delivery costs, with fixed payroll unchanged.

$60,000 one-time implementation / $47,160~15.3 months paybackOnly after the recurring benefit is fully achieved; excludes ramp time and financing.

If there is no additional demand and payroll does not fall, the revenue benefit disappears. The intervention may still reduce overtime or improve service, but that needs separate evidence. Conversely, if the bottleneck is licensed judgment, removing administrative load from that person can be worth more than speeding a task with abundant capacity.

A knowledge-transfer service needs to measure its own labor too. Expert interviews, permissions, case validation, integration and updates can dominate model cost. The repeatability question is whether a provider can reuse a process and evaluation method across similar businesses without pretending their operating rules are identical.

06 / Opportunity hypotheses

Make expertise more available without eroding it.

Potential businesses and their disproof tests
Need / likely customerPotential offerWhat would make it fail
Technician productivity / service operatorAn integrated assistant for preparation, documentation and proven troubleshooting, with expert escalation.Travel, parts availability or demand dominates; information access is not the constraint.
Expert succession / owner or acquirerCapture and validate decision rules during an actual transition, then maintain them in the workflow.Recorded knowledge is not used, or successor performance does not improve.
Reviewer capacity / professional firmStandardize preparation, route exceptions and preserve evidence for accountable review.More generated work increases review burden or error cost.
Apprenticeship / employer or training providerCase-based practice with explanation, feedback and unaided assessment.Better scores do not predict job performance, or employers will not fund training.
Outcome delivery / capacity-constrained businessOperate a narrowly defined process with software and qualified human supervision.Custom work, liability and staffing consume the margin.

Likely beneficiaries include employers that can combine experienced staff with well-designed systems, and specialists whose judgment becomes more widely usable. Routine drafting providers face price pressure if customers can perform that step themselves. Experienced people do not automatically lose value: their contribution can move toward exception handling, training and validation. These are directional hypotheses, not occupation-level forecasts.

07 / What would change the view

Test transfer, not documentation volume.

Evidence to collect next
QuestionMeasureThesis weakens if…
What is actually scarce?Observe workload, queues, vacancies, turnover, rejected demand and local compensation.Better pay or scheduling solves the problem more cheaply.
Does knowledge transfer?Compare unaided performance on new cases before and after training, with blind quality review.Answers sound better but first-time-right performance does not improve.
Does capacity turn into output?Completed work, repeat visits, customer outcomes and total human hours.Savings merely move work to reviewers or fail to increase usable capacity.
Will expertise remain current?Update frequency, expert participation and failures caused by obsolete rules.Maintenance requires more scarce expert time than the system saves.
Does the next generation learn?Track explanation quality, independent diagnosis and progression over months.Short-term output rises while independent competence deteriorates.

The durable trend is the need to renew and distribute expertise. The speed of automation and the availability of workers will vary by occupation and place. A company thesis should therefore start with a specific operational constraint, rather than a national “labor shortage” headline.
